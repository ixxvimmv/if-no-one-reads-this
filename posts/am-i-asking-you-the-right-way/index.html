Am I Asking You the Right Way?

Sometimes, the hardest thing to ask is for permission to love someone properly.

homelove

It's funny how easily I show my love to you. Maybe it's because I've never known how to love halfway. Maybe it's because I don't believe in giving someone I love the bare minimum. Or maybe this is simply the way I know how to love you—with everything I have, without holding myself back.

I want you to feel it in the little things I do, in the words I write, in the time I give you, and in every little reminder that you are loved. I want you to feel it so deeply that you never have to doubt yourself, question yourself, or wonder whether you are worthy of all the love I give you.

Because you are, my love.

You are more than worthy of everything.

I never want you to feel like you have to earn my affection. I don't want you to wonder if you're being too much, if you're asking for too much, or if there's a limit to how much love you deserve. There isn't. At least not from me.

I never settle for less, and I never love less. If I love, I love more.

More than yesterday.

More than I thought I could.

More than I know how to put into words.

And even though I've been courting you—or at least, I think I have—am I already? I haven't even properly asked you. So, can I?

Can I have your permission to court you?

Can I intentionally pursue you, not because I want to rush whatever this is between us, but because I want to take my time getting to know you even more? Can I continue showing you how much you mean to me, without expecting anything from you before you're ready to give it?

If you let me, I won't promise to be the perfect suitor to you. I don't think I could ever make that promise because I'm still learning, still figuring out how to love you in the ways you deserve. But I can promise that I'll always try. I'll give you the best of what I can—not because I expect perfection from you in return, but because you are someone worth making an effort for.

I'll learn your little habits. I'll remember the things you casually mention. I'll listen to the stories you've already told me a hundred times. I'll celebrate your smallest victories and stay beside you through the days that don't feel like victories at all.

I won't promise that I'll always know what to say.

But I'll promise to listen.

I won't promise that I'll always know how to fix things.

But I'll promise to stay.

And I won't promise that I'll never make mistakes.

But I will promise that when I do, I'll acknowledge them, learn from them, and try to do better.

Because if you give me the chance to court you, I don't want to simply impress you.

I want to know you.

I want to understand you.

I want to love you intentionally.

Every word I've written, every little thing I've done, every time I've stayed up just to talk to you, every time I've listened to you tell me about your day—none of it was something I did simply because I wanted to impress you.

I did it because it's you.

I know you know that.

I know you can feel how much I love you, even in the things I don't explicitly say. I know you can probably see it in the way I remember the smallest things about you, in the way I get excited when I see something that reminds me of you, in the way I listen to your stories even when they have no ending, and in the way I always seem to find another reason to stay a little longer whenever we're together.

I told you before—this is one of the ways I court you.

I write you letters.

I put my feelings into words because sometimes saying "I love you" doesn't feel like enough. Sometimes three words can't hold everything I want you to know.

So I write.

I write about the way you make me feel. I write about the things I notice about you. I write about your voice, your laughter, your stories, your little habits, your favorite color, your dreams, your tired days, and all the little pieces of you that somehow became important to me.

I know writing letters might seem like the bare minimum.

Maybe it is.

But if writing you one letter makes you feel loved for a moment, then I'd rather write you hundreds.

Thousands, even.

I'll fill pages with you if that's what it takes to remind you how much you mean to me.

I'll write about you on the days when you're happy and on the days when you're exhausted. I'll write when you accomplish something you've been working toward, and I'll write when you feel like you haven't accomplished enough. I'll write when you need reassurance, when you need someone to remind you that you're doing well, and even when you simply need to be reminded that someone is proud of you.

Because I don't want my love to exist only in the moments when everything is easy.

I want you to feel it in the ordinary days, too.

In the random messages.

In the late-night calls.

In the "have you eaten?" texts.

In the "get home safe."

In the "I'm proud of you."

In the little things that may seem insignificant but quietly say, I'm thinking about you.

Maybe that's what I mean when I say I don't know how to love less.

I don't know how to look at someone I love and decide that they only deserve a certain amount of affection. I don't know how to count the times I've said "I love you" or keep track of how much effort I've given. I don't want to love you like I'm keeping score.

I just want to love you.

And if someday you forget how worthy you are, I'll remind you.

If you ever question whether you're enough, I'll remind you.

If you ever wonder whether you're lovable on the days when you're tired, messy, overwhelmed, or unsure of yourself, I'll remind you.

You don't have to be the best version of yourself for me to love you.

You don't have to have everything figured out.

You don't have to be perfect.

You just have to be you.

And somehow, that's always been more than enough for me.

So yes, I'm still courting you.

I'm still trying to make you fall for me.

I'm still writing letters and finding little ways to make you smile.

But somewhere along the way, I think these letters stopped being only about making you mine.

They became my way of saying:

I see you.

I appreciate you.

I'm proud of you.

I'm here.

And most importantly—

You are loved.

Maybe one day, when you look back at all these letters, you'll realize that I wasn't simply trying to win your heart.

I was trying to show you what it feels like to be loved without having to question whether you deserve it.

I want you to look at my love and never feel like you have to compete for it. I want you to feel safe enough to be yourself around me. I want you to know that you can be soft, loud, tired, emotional, excited, messy, or completely unsure of yourself, and I will still see you as someone worth loving.

And if I have to write a thousand more letters just to make you believe that, then I'll gladly write every single one.

Because if loving you means filling pages with everything my heart has been trying to say, then give me all the paper in the world.

I'll write until I run out of pages.

And if I somehow run out of words, I'll find another way to show you.

I'll never run out of reasons to write about you.

I'll never run out of things to love about you.

And I'll never want to love you any less.

Because if you let me, I don't just want to be someone who once courted you.

I want to be someone who, even years from now, still finds new reasons to choose you.

Written in the quiet hours, mostly for me—and now, for you too.
Cathlynn.

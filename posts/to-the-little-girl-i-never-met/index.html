To the Little Girl I Never Met

She endured so much just to become the woman I get to love today.

adulthoodhomelove
I'm not just loving you for who you are and who you are becoming; I'm also loving the little girl inside you who has been through so much just to become the person you are today. I may not have known her when she was younger, and I may never know every story she carried quietly, but I wish I could have been there for her, too. I wish I could have told her that she didn't deserve every difficult thing she had to go through, that she didn't have to understand everything so soon, and that one day, she would grow into someone worth being proud of.

I wonder what she was like before the world taught her how to be strong. I wonder what made her laugh uncontrollably, what dreams she had before she started worrying about whether they were possible, and what little things made her happy before life became complicated. I wonder how many times she had to wipe her own tears, how many things she kept to herself because she didn't know who to tell, and how many moments she had to pretend that she was okay when she really wasn't.

I wish I could go back and sit beside that little girl, even if only for a moment, and tell her that she would make it here. I would tell her that the things she was going through would not be the end of her story. I would tell her that one day, she would grow up to become someone who knows how to love, how to care, how to dream, and how to keep going even when life gives her every reason to stop.

Maybe she wouldn't believe me at first.

Maybe she would wonder how someone could possibly know that things would get better.

But I would still tell her.

Because I know the person she became.

I know the woman who still wakes up every day and tries again. I know the woman who gets tired but continues anyway. I know the woman who spends so much of herself caring for other people, even when she has her own battles quietly happening in the background. I know the woman who sometimes doubts herself but still finds a way to move forward.

And knowing her makes me wish I could have known the little girl, too.

I want to love every version of you—not just the version standing in front of me now, but every version of you that had to exist before her. The child who was still learning what the world was, the girl who was trying to understand herself, the teenager who was figuring out how to survive things she never thought she would have to face, and the woman who eventually learned how to carry all those experiences with her.

None of those versions of you disappeared.

They became part of you.

And I think that's why I want to be gentle with you. Because I know there is a younger version of you somewhere inside, still carrying memories that I may never fully understand. There are parts of your past that I cannot change, wounds I cannot undo, and moments I cannot go back to protect you from.

But I can love the person who came out of them.

I can remind you that you don't have to be strong every second. I can listen when you need to talk about things that still hurt. I can celebrate the things that make you happy. I can stay beside you when you're tired. And when you forget how far you've come, I can remind you.

Because sometimes, I don't think you realize just how much that little girl accomplished by making it this far.

She survived days she probably thought she couldn't.

She grew through things that could have made her bitter.

She learned how to love despite the things that hurt her.

And somehow, she became you.

I'm not only loving the woman you became; I'm loving the little girl who had to survive long enough for me to meet her.

I think that's one of the most beautiful things about loving someone: you don't simply fall in love with who they are today. You begin to understand the pieces of their past that shaped them, the experiences that made them who they are, and the younger versions of them that still live quietly beneath everything they've become.

So if there are days when you feel like you're not doing enough, I hope you remember the little girl you once were.

She didn't know where life would take her.

She didn't know what she would become.

She didn't know all the people she would meet, all the dreams she would chase, all the things she would accomplish, or all the love she would eventually find.

But she kept going anyway.

And look at you now.

You're still here.

You're still dreaming.

You're still becoming.

And I'm so proud of you for that.

I hope you learn to look at yourself with the same gentleness that I see you with. I hope you stop being so hard on the person you are now for not having everything figured out. You have already spent so much of your life trying to become someone you could be proud of.

Maybe it's time to realize that you already are.

And if I could tell that little girl one more thing, I would tell her that she did well. She did more than well.

She became someone deeply loved.

She became someone worth staying for.

She became someone whose stories I want to hear, whose dreams I want to witness, whose happiness I want to celebrate, and whose difficult days I want to sit through.

I may never be able to meet the little girl you once were, but I can love the woman she became.

And maybe, in loving you gently today, I can give a little love to the younger you who deserved it all along.
Written in the quiet hours, mostly for me—and now, for you too.
Cathlynn.

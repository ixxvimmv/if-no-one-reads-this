Will Always Be a Shelter, Never a Storm

I cannot promise a life without storms, but I can promise not to become one.

lettershomelove

The Ridleys said, “I'll be more of a shelter and less of a storm.” And in this world full of doubts, pressure, stress, and noise—where gentleness sometimes feels so rare—let me remind you that I'll be your shelter. Let me be your pahinga. Let me be the person you can come home to when the world becomes too loud. Let me be your comfort person, the one you can run to when you don't know where else to go.

I don't want to be another source of heaviness in your life. There are already enough things outside our control that make life difficult. There are already enough expectations, responsibilities, worries, and days that leave us exhausted. So when you come to me, I want you to feel like you can finally put everything down, even just for a while.

You don't have to be strong with me.

You don't have to pretend that you're okay.

You don't have to carefully choose your words because you're afraid I'll misunderstand you.

You can tell me what you're feeling. You can tell me what hurt you. You can tell me when you're angry, when you're tired, when you're scared, or when you simply don't know what you're feeling at all.

I want to know you in those moments, too.

And I promise that someday, when we have a home of our own, I don't want it to be filled with storms. I don't want our home to become another place where you have to walk on eggshells, afraid that one wrong word will turn into an argument. I don't want shouting to become our language, or blame to become our way of solving things.

I want our home to be a shelter.

A place where you can breathe.

A place where you can express yourself without fear of being judged.

A place where you can be completely yourself without worrying that you'll become too much.

A place where we can talk about things even when they're difficult, where we choose understanding before anger, and where we learn how to meet each other halfway instead of trying to prove who's right.

And when we do hurt each other—because I know that loving someone doesn't mean we'll never make mistakes—I hope we remember that we're on the same side.

I don't want us to fight against each other.

I want us to fight for each other.

I want us to learn how to apologize without pride getting in the way. I want us to learn how to listen without immediately preparing a defense. I want us to be able to say, “That hurt me,” without turning it into an accusation and “I'm sorry,” without feeling like we've lost something by admitting we were wrong.

Because love shouldn't feel like a battlefield.

It should feel like coming home.

And if one day you come home after the hardest day you've had and all you want is silence, I'll sit beside you in silence. If you want to talk, I'll listen. If you want to cry, I'll hold you. If you need space, I'll give you space without making you feel like you're being abandoned.

I want to learn the difference between when you need my words and when you simply need my presence.

I want to be the person who knows when to say something and when to just stay.

Because I don't want to promise you a life without storms. I know I can't control everything that happens outside our home. There will still be difficult days. There will still be problems we can't immediately solve. There will be moments when we're both tired, frustrated, or overwhelmed.

But I can promise you this:

I don't want to become the storm you have to escape from. I want to be the place you can escape to.

I want our home to be filled with laughter, sleepy conversations, random stories, quiet mornings, late-night talks, and the kind of silence that doesn't need to be explained.

I want there to be a place where you can leave your worries at the door.

Where your tiredness is allowed to exist.

Where your dreams are encouraged.

Where your mistakes are forgiven.

Where your feelings have a place.

Where your voice is heard.

Where your heart is safe.

And maybe someday, when you look around that home, you'll realize that we built something neither of us had to be afraid of.

Something gentle.

Something warm.

Something that feels like us.

So until that day comes, let me start with the little things.

Let me listen when you need to rant.

Let me hold you when you're tired.

Let me remind you to rest when you've forgotten that you're allowed to.

Let me celebrate your victories, no matter how small.

Let me sit beside you through the days that don't go according to plan.

Let me be your pahinga after a long day.

And when the world becomes too much, come home to me.

Not necessarily to a place.

Not yet.

But to me.

Because if I get the privilege of loving you, I want you to know that you will never have to wonder whether there is a soft place for you to land.

There will always be a place for you here.

And if the world insists on being a storm, then let me spend my life learning how to be your shelter.

Written in the quiet hours, mostly for me—and now, for you too.
Cathlynn.

If My Younger Self Read This

The person you’re trying so hard to become has been quietly cheering for you all along.

growing upadulthoodjournallife

Dear younger me, 

You always believed that one day, everything would make sense.

You thought there would come a morning when you’d wake up and suddenly know who you were, what you wanted, and where you belonged. You imagined adulthood as a destination—a place where confusion no longer existed.

I'm writing to tell you that it doesn’t quite happen that way.

You don’t magically become fearless. You don’t stop making mistakes. You don’t suddenly understand life just because you’ve grown older.

But somehow, you become softer with yourself.

You'll spend years trying to earn everyone’s approval before realizing that no amount of validation can replace the peace that comes from accepting yourself. You'll overthink conversations that everyone else has already forgotten. You'll apologize for taking up space, even when you’ve done nothing wrong.

I wish I could tell you not to.

not because life will be easy, but because you’ll eventually learn that your worth was never something other people had the authority to decide.

There will be friendships that feel permanent until they aren’t.

People you thought would stay forever will quietly become strangers. At first, you’ll think it’s your fault—that maybe if you had been funnier, kinder, quieter, louder, or simply “more,” they would have stayed.

They wouldn’t have.

some people are only meant to walk beside you for a chapter, not the whole story. their leaving isn’t always a reflection of your value. sometimes it’s simply how life unfolds.

You'll also discover that growing up means grieving versions of yourself.

You'll miss the child who laughed without worrying about how she looked. You'll miss the teenager who believed love could fix everything. You'll miss old dreams that no longer fit the person you’re becoming.

It's okay to miss them.

You don’t have to hate who you used to be in order to appreciate who you’re becoming.

And here’s something no one tells you: healing isn’t dramatic.

It's choosing to get out of bed on difficult mornings. It's learning not to send the message you typed in the middle of the night. It's forgiving yourself for things you didn’t know then. It's noticing that the memories no longer hurt the way they once did.

Healing often arrives so quietly that you don’t realize you’ve changed until one day, something that used to break you barely touches your heart.

You'll learn that love isn’t supposed to make you shrink.

The right people won’t ask you to become less emotional, less ambitious, or less yourself just to make them comfortable. Real love makes room for your honesty. Real friendship doesn’t make you question whether you’re enough.

Please don’t spend your life chasing people who have already decided not to choose you.

Choose yourself, too.

And while you’re at it, keep writing.

Write even when no one is reading.

Write when you’re happy, because joy deserves to be remembered.

Write when you’re heartbroken, because pain becomes lighter once it has somewhere to go.

write because one day you’ll look back at these words and realize they were never just pages—they were proof that you survived every version of yourself.

You won’t have all the answers.

Honestly, I hope you never do.

because life isn’t measured by how quickly you figure everything out. It's measured by how willing you are to keep learning, keep loving, and keep beginning again.

So be patient.

One day you’ll understand that becoming isn’t about finding yourself.

It's about creating yourself, little by little, through every choice you make.

And if you ever feel lost, remember this:

the person you’re trying so hard to become has been quietly cheering for you all along.

With love,

Your older self.

Written in the quiet hours, mostly for me—and now, for you too.
Cathlynn.

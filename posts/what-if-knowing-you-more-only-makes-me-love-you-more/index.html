What If Knowing You More Only Makes Me Love You More?

Maybe every new piece of you I discover is just another reason to fall.

lettershomelove

I keep seeing the phrase, “Does knowing me more lead to loving me less?”  And I remember that I even wrote something related to it before, but back then, it was all about me. This time, I want to rephrase it and make it about you. Maybe it should be, “Knowing you more could never make me love you less.” Or perhaps, “Knowing you more will never lead me to love you less.”

You see, my love, it's just a single phrase, but there is such a huge meaning behind it.

Because for me, knowing you more isn't simply knowing your favorite color, your favorite food, the songs you listen to, or the little things that make you smile. It's knowing who you are and who you're becoming. It's learning the person behind the things you show everyone else. It's understanding the parts of you that you don't always talk about. It's learning what makes you happy, what makes you uncomfortable, what makes you feel safe, what makes you anxious, what you need when you're tired, what you want when you're dreaming, and what you need from someone when life becomes too heavy.

It's almost like studying you—not in a way that makes you something to be analyzed, but in a way that makes me want to understand every little piece of you.

Your wants.

Your needs.

Your favorites.

Your dislikes.

Your habits.

Your little quirks.

The things that make you laugh.

The things that make you quiet.

The things you get excited about.

The things you overthink.

The dreams you're chasing.

The fears you don't always say out loud.

Even the little things you probably don't think anyone notices.

I want to know them.

Not because I want to memorize you like a list of facts, but because I want to understand how to love you better.

I want to know what kind of comfort you need when you've had a difficult day. I want to know when you need someone to listen and when you simply need someone to stay beside you. I want to know the difference between your “I'm okay” that genuinely means you're okay and your “I'm okay” that secretly means you don't want to talk about it yet.

I want to know the things that make you feel loved, not just the things that make you feel noticed.

And the more I know, the more I realize that knowing someone doesn't make them less lovable.

It makes them more human.

And somehow, that makes me love you even more.

Because I don't want to love some perfect version of you that exists only in my imagination. I want to love the real you—the person who gets tired, gets frustrated, makes mistakes, changes her mind, has bad days, overthinks sometimes, laughs at the most random things, and still continues to become the person she's meant to be.

I want to know you beyond the version of yourself that is easiest to love.

I want to know you completely, or at least as completely as you are willing to let me.

And in the end, after knowing you more, I know one thing for certain:

I will never, ever love you less.

Because, like I said in one of my previous letters, if I love, I love more.

And loving you feels like you've already become my other half—not because I believe I am incomplete without you, but because somehow, you've become a part of my life that I cannot imagine simply removing from it.

You've become part of my ordinary days.

Part of my thoughts.

Part of my little routines.

Part of the things I look forward to.

Part of the stories I want to tell.

Part of the person I think about when something makes me smile.

And somewhere along the way, you've become someone I don't want to lose.

Not because I want to hold you so tightly that you can't be yourself, but because I value the person you are and the place you've found in my life.

I want you to grow.

I want you to change.

I want you to discover new things about yourself.

I want you to become someone you've never imagined you could be.

And when you do, I want to know that version of you, too.

Because loving you doesn't mean loving only who you are today.

It means being willing to meet all the versions of you that tomorrow brings.

So think about that phrase again:

“Does knowing me more lead to loving me less? ”

Now turn it around.

Does knowing you more lead me to loving you less?

No.

Not even close.

If anything, every new thing I learn about you gives me another reason to stay curious, another reason to appreciate you, another reason to fall a little deeper.

So, do I think knowing you more is enough?

No.

I don't think I'll ever feel like I've known enough.

Because there will always be another story I haven't heard, another dream you haven't told me about, another little habit I haven't noticed yet, another part of your heart you haven't shown me.

And I want to discover them.

Slowly.

Gently.

Without rushing you.

I would like—no, I mean, I would love—to know you more.

And if knowing you more means I get to love you more, then let me keep learning.

Let me keep listening.

Let me keep discovering.

Let me keep falling.

Because I don't want to reach a point where I can say, “I already know everything about you.”

I want to reach a point where, even after years of knowing you, I can still look at you and think:

“There is still so much of you left for me to love.”

And maybe that's the real difference.

I don't want to know you more so I can decide whether you're worth loving.

I want to know you more because I already know you're worth loving—and I want to discover every reason why.

Written in the quiet hours, mostly for me—and now, for you too.
Cathlynn.

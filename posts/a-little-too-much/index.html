A Little Too Much

Maybe I worry this much because somewhere along the way, you became someone I couldn't help but care about.

homeloveself-reflection

Worrying about someone is normal, I know. But why do I worry so much with this strange hint of guilt that I can't seem to shake? It's like I don't have the right to worry, especially when I know you're okay, or at least safe until you get home. I know you're capable of taking care of yourself. I know you know what you're doing. So why does a part of me still feel uneasy whenever I don't hear from you?

Maybe I'm not even worried.

Maybe I'm just upset because I'm worried.

Or maybe I'm worried because I care too much.

I don't know anymore.

It's confusing when your mind knows there's nothing to be afraid of, but your heart still refuses to settle down. I can tell myself a hundred times that you're fine, that you'll be home soon, that there's no reason to overthink anything, and yet I still find myself checking my phone, wondering if you've gotten home, wondering how your day went, wondering if something happened that you haven't told me about.

And then I feel guilty for even feeling that way.

Because who am I to worry this much?

I'm not supposed to know every little detail of your day. I'm not supposed to expect constant updates or need reassurance every time you're out. You have your own life, your own responsibilities, your own things to do. I understand that. I really do.

But understanding something doesn't always make the feeling disappear.

Sometimes I wish I could turn it off.

I wish I could simply think, "She's okay," and leave it at that. I wish I could stop myself from looking at the time and wondering why it's taking longer than usual. I wish I could stop imagining every possible reason why you haven't replied yet, even when I know most of them are probably nothing.

Maybe you're busy.

Maybe you're tired.

Maybe you're talking to someone.

Maybe you simply put your phone down and forgot about it.

And that's okay.

I know it is.

Still, there I am, quietly waiting for that little notification that tells me you're home.

Not because I need you to explain yourself.

Not because I want to keep track of where you are.

Not because I don't trust you.

I think I just want to know that you're safe.

That's all.

Maybe that's where the guilt comes from. I worry, but I don't want my worry to become a burden to you. I care, but I don't want my care to feel like a responsibility you have to carry. I miss you, but I don't want you to feel like you always have to be available just because I happen to be waiting.

So I keep most of it to myself.

I tell myself to relax.

I tell myself you're okay.

I tell myself that caring about someone doesn't mean I have to know where they are every second of the day.

And eventually, I realize that maybe love isn't about having control over someone's safety. Maybe it's simply accepting that you can't always protect the people you love, no matter how badly you want to.

All I can do is hope you're okay.

Hope you get home safely.

Hope you had a good day.

Hope that when you finally get home, you're able to rest and forget about everything that exhausted you.

And maybe when that notification finally comes, I'll smile quietly to myself and think, "There you are."

Maybe I'll pretend I wasn't worried at all.

Maybe I'll act like I wasn't checking my phone every few minutes.

Maybe I'll simply ask, "Did you get home safely?"

And when you say yes, I'll finally breathe a little easier.

Because maybe that's what happens when you care about someone deeply enough.

Their safety becomes a small part of your peace.

Their silence becomes something you notice.

Their presence becomes something you look forward to.

And their "I'm home" becomes something that can instantly quiet a mind that has spent the last hour making up a hundred different possibilities.

I don't think I can explain exactly when it happened.

I don't know when your well-being became something my heart instinctively worried about.

Maybe it happened slowly, somewhere between our late-night calls, your stories about your day, the little things you tell me, and all the moments we've shared.

Maybe I just started caring before I realized I was already caring this much.

And maybe that's why I'm worried.

Not because I think you're incapable of taking care of yourself.

Not because I don't trust you.

But because somewhere along the way, you became someone I genuinely want to see safe, happy, and home at the end of every day.

So maybe I'm not upset.

Maybe I'm not guilty for worrying.

Maybe I'm just learning what it feels like to love someone enough that a simple "I'm home" can mean more to me than you realize.

And if that's what caring too much feels like, then I suppose I'll have to learn how to carry it gently.

Written in the quiet hours, mostly for me—and now, for you too.
Cathlynn.

Tell Me Everything

For the nurse who spends her days caring for everyone else, and deserves someone to listen to her, too.

homeloveprose

I used to think that loving someone meant being there for the happiest moments—the celebrations, the late-night laughter, the little victories, and all the beautiful things that make life feel lighter. I thought love was supposed to be loud sometimes, something you could easily recognize in grand gestures and carefully chosen words.

But the more I get to know you, the more I realize that maybe love can also be quiet.

Maybe love is sitting on the other end of the phone while you tell me about your day. Maybe it's listening to the same story you've already told me before and still letting you tell it again. Maybe it's remembering the little things you casually mention and asking about them later. Maybe it's simply being there when the excitement fades and all that's left is exhaustion.

And maybe, for me, loving you means choosing to stay through all of it.

I witnessed all your struggles every day, and I still choose to love you gently. Like what I've said, life has already been so hard on us. At the very least, if listening to your rants and hearing about what happened throughout your day—whether something good or bad happened along the way—is what you need, then I'll gladly be here to listen.

It doesn't matter if you have so much to yap about. I don't mind how long your stories get, how many times you repeat the same thing, or how many little details you want to tell me. All I think about is that maybe you just need someone to listen to your rants, your stories, your frustrations, or even the most random thoughts that cross your mind.

And I don't mind if you're tired, overwhelmed, irritated, or having one of those days when everything seems to go wrong. I don't mind if all you want to do is complain about how exhausting your day was. I don't need you to make your stories sound interesting, and you don't have to pretend that everything is okay when it isn't. You can tell me about the good days, but you can also give me the bad ones.

I want to know both.

I want to hear about the little things that made you smile, the things that made you laugh, the moments that made your heart feel light, and even the things that made you want to cry at the end of the day. I want to know what made you happy and what made you upset. I want to hear you say, "I had such a terrible day," and still be able to remind you that tomorrow might be a little kinder.

Because loving you, for me, isn't only about being there when everything is beautiful.

It's also about staying when things aren't.

I know I can't take away every difficult thing you have to face. I can't magically make your responsibilities disappear, make your exhaustion go away, or fix every problem that comes your way. Sometimes, all I can really do is listen. Sometimes, all I can offer is my time, my attention, and a place where you can let everything out without worrying about being too much.

And if that's all you need from me, then that's enough.

You never have to apologize for talking too much. You never have to wonder if you're bothering me. You never have to filter yourself just because you think your problems are too small or your stories are too long. If something matters to you, then it matters to me too—not because I have to understand every little thing, but because I love you enough to want to understand the things that matter to you.

I want to be the person you can call after a long day and say, "I have a story to tell about what happened to me earlier," without hesitation.

I want to be the person you can tell about the smallest victory you had that day, even if it seems insignificant to everyone else. I want to celebrate the little things with you, too, because I know that sometimes the smallest wins are the ones that keep us going.

And if you come to me with nothing but exhaustion, I'll stay.

If you come to me with a hundred stories, I'll listen.

If you come to me laughing, I'll laugh with you.

If you come to me crying, I'll stay until you've said everything you need to say.

You don't always have to be strong around me.

You don't always have to be the nurse who knows how to care for everyone else. You don't always have to be the one who knows what to do, what to say, or how to make everything better.

Sometimes, you can just be you.

And let someone take care of you for a while.

Maybe that's one of the ways I want to love you—with patience, with understanding, and with gentleness. Not because I think you're fragile, but because I know how much you carry every day, even when you don't talk about it.

So if life gets too loud, come to me.

If your day gets too heavy, tell me about it.

If you have too much to say, yap as much as you want.

I'll be here.

I'll listen to the stories you've told me a hundred times and the ones you're telling me for the first time. I'll listen to the things that make you happy and the things that make you want to scream. I'll listen even when there isn't really a point to the story, because sometimes you don't need someone to solve anything.

Sometimes, you just need someone who stays long enough to hear you say it all.

And I want to be that someone for you.

Because I don't only want to love the version of you that is happy, well-rested, and having a good day.

I want to love you on the days when you're exhausted, frustrated, messy, emotional, and completely overwhelmed, too.

I want to know you in your softness and in your chaos.

And no matter how difficult the day has been, I hope you always remember that you have someone who will gladly listen to you at the end of it.

Even if all you have to say is, "Today was terrible."

I'll still answer, "Okay, tell me everything."

Written in the quiet hours, mostly for me—and now, for you too.
Cathlynn.

Does Every “I Love You” Have a Different Meaning?

Because sometimes three words can carry an entire heart.

lettershomelove

I love you.

I love you so much.

I love you more.

Is there really any difference between those three?

Maybe there is.

Maybe there isn't.

But to me, those three words aren't just words. They carry different feelings, different depths, and different ways of saying what my heart sometimes struggles to explain. To some people, “I love you” might be nothing more than a phrase they say out of habit. Some people say it so easily, as if those words don't hold any weight. As if they're just three ordinary words that can be said whenever the moment feels right.

But I don't see them that way.

When I tell you “I love you,” I mean it.

I mean that I choose you.

I mean that I care about the little things you tell me, even the things you think are insignificant. I mean that your happiness matters to me. I mean that your bad days matter, too. I mean that I want to know how your day went, whether it was the best day you've had or one you'd rather forget.

When I tell you “I love you so much,” it's because sometimes “I love you” doesn't feel big enough to contain everything I feel for you.

It's the love that grows in the middle of ordinary conversations.

It's the love I feel when I hear your voice.

It's the love behind every late-night call, every random message, every little thing that reminds me of you.

It's the love that makes me smile when I see your name on my screen.

It's the love that makes me remember your favorite color.

It's the love that makes me want to write another letter even when I've already written so many.

And then there's “I love you more.”

Maybe you think I'm simply trying to win an argument whenever I say it.

Maybe it's just our little way of teasing each other.

But sometimes, when I say “I love you more,” I don't mean more than you love me.

I mean that I want to keep finding more ways to love you.

More patience.

More understanding.

More effort.

More reassurance.

More time.

More little things that make you feel cared for.

More reasons for you to never question whether you're loved.

Because for me, loving you isn't supposed to be a competition where one of us has to prove that we love the other more.

It's not about keeping score.

It's not about who said it first or who said it last.

It's simply about giving love freely and letting it grow.

And maybe that's the difference between those three.

"I love you" is me telling you how I feel.

"I love you so much" is me trying to tell you that my feelings are bigger than the words can hold.

And "I love you more" is my promise that I don't want my love for you to remain exactly where it is. I want it to grow with us.

I want to love you more gently when you're tired.

More patiently when we're struggling to understand each other.

More intentionally when life gets busy.

More loudly when you need reassurance.

And more quietly when you don't need words at all.

Because I don't want “I love you” to become something you hear so often that it loses its meaning.

I want you to feel it.

I want you to see it in the way I treat you.

I want you to hear it in the way I listen.

I want you to find it in the little things I remember.

I want you to recognize it in the way I stay.

Because anyone can say “I love you.”

But I want my actions to make you believe it.

I want my love to be something you can rest in, not something you constantly have to question.

So if I tell you “I love you” a hundred times, I hope you never think I'm simply repeating myself.

I'm reminding you.

I'm reminding you that you are loved today.

And tomorrow.

And on the days when you're happy.

And on the days when you're tired.

And on the days when you don't feel particularly lovable.

Especially then.

So maybe there isn't really a difference between “I love you,” “I love you so much,” and “I love you more.”

Maybe they're simply different ways of saying the same thing:

I love you, and I mean it every single time.

And if words ever become insufficient, I'll find another way to tell you.

I'll write it.

I'll show it.

I'll stay.

I'll choose you.

And I'll keep finding new ways to say those three little words until you never have to wonder what they mean when they come from me.

Written in the quiet hours, mostly for me—and now, for you too.
Cathlynn.

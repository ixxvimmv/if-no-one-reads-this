The Days I Started Counting

On meeting you, choosing you, and never wanting to stop knowing you.

lettershomelove

Does counting days or months matter?

I'm not really sure if it does. I used to think time was just time—something that passes whether we pay attention to it or not. But then I caught myself counting.

I started counting from the day I asked if I could court you. Two months since that question left my mouth. Three months since the night we first met. Three months since you were still a stranger whose name I didn't know would eventually become something I would look for in the middle of my day.

It's strange how quickly those numbers arrived.

Two months.

Three months.

They sound small when I say them out loud. Almost insignificant compared to how long people usually take to know someone. But when I think about everything that has happened within those months, I don't think of them as small anymore.

I think of all the ordinary days that somehow became worth remembering.

The conversations that stretched longer than they were supposed to. The moments when I learned something new about you without even realizing I was keeping it somewhere in my mind. The things you've said in passing that stayed with me long after the conversation ended. The parts of your personality that slowly became familiar—not because I tried to study you, but because paying attention to you started feeling natural.

Maybe that's why I started counting.

Not because I need a number to prove how long I've loved you.

Not because two months or three months suddenly makes what I feel more valid.

I think I count because sometimes I can't believe how much can happen between one date and another.

Three months ago, I didn't know you.

Two months ago, I asked if you would let me try to know you in a different way.

And now, here I am, somehow remembering things about you that I never thought I would care enough to remember.

I remember the way you phrase certain things. The little habits you've mentioned. The preferences you've casually revealed. The things you get excited about. The things that make you tired. The moments when you become quieter. The moments when your personality fills the entire conversation.

I don't think I intentionally memorized any of it.

It just stayed.

That's the strange thing about getting attached to someone. You don't always notice when the details start becoming familiar. There isn't a specific moment when you realize, Oh, I know this about her now.

One day, you simply know.

You know what kind of answer she might give. You recognize the little changes in the way she talks. You remember something she mentioned weeks ago without having to make an effort. You notice when something sounds different from usual.

And suddenly, you realize you've been paying attention all along.

I didn't just memorize you.

I memorized the person you are.

And maybe that's different.

Because people change. The version of you I met three months ago isn't going to be exactly the same person I know months from now. There will be things about you that shift with time, things you'll outgrow, things you'll discover about yourself, things you might eventually see differently.

And I don't want to hold you to the version I first met.

I want to have the privilege of seeing what comes after her.

Who you'll become when your life changes. What you'll believe in years from now. What you'll eventually want for yourself. Which dreams will stay and which ones you'll replace with something you never expected to want.

Maybe that's why the idea of knowing you feels so much more exciting to me than the idea of having you completely figured out.

Because I don't think I ever want to reach a point where I can say, I already know everything about you.

That sounds less like love to me and more like the end of curiosity.

And I don't want my curiosity about you to end.

I don't want to know you completely. I want to know you continuously—to keep meeting the versions of you that time brings, and to never run out of reasons to be curious about the person you're becoming.

I won't lie and say that memorizing someone is easy. Sometimes I probably get things wrong. Sometimes I misunderstand. Sometimes I think I know what you're thinking and realize I was completely wrong.

But even that doesn't make me want to stop.

If anything, it reminds me that you're a person, not a puzzle I'm supposed to solve.

You're allowed to surprise me.

You're allowed to change your mind.

You're allowed to become someone I didn't predict.

You're allowed to have parts of yourself that I haven't reached yet.

And maybe I'll always be a little curious about those parts.

I think I'm more in love with the possibility of knowing you than with the comfort of thinking I already do.

Because knowing you isn't supposed to have an ending.

There will always be another conversation, another season, another version of you I haven't encountered yet. There will be days when I'll understand you immediately and days when I won't. There will be things you'll tell me years from now that I couldn't possibly know today.

And somehow, I like that.

I like that there is still so much life ahead of us that neither of us can predict.

So yes, maybe counting does matter to me after all.

Not because I'm keeping score.

Not because I'm waiting for a certain number of months to make what we have feel more important.

I count because each number reminds me that time is actually moving.

Three months since we met.

Two months since I asked you.

And however many days have passed since then, I know I've spent them becoming more certain that knowing you is something I never want to take for granted.

Maybe someday, I'll stop counting.

Maybe the months will blur together, and I'll forget the exact number of days between one milestone and another.

But I hope I'll never forget what those numbers meant when they were still new.

Three months was when I realized a stranger could become someone familiar.

Two months was when I chose to take a chance on whatever this could become.

And today is another day I get to know you a little more than I did yesterday.

Maybe that's enough.

Maybe I don't need to know how many days it will take.

I just know that, for as long as I'm given the chance, I want to keep meeting every version of you that time introduces me to.

Written in the quiet hours, mostly for me—and now, for you too.
Cathlynn.

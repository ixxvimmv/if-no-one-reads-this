Does A Yearner Poet Exist?

prosecreative writing
Yes, it does. A yearner poet is me—I am the yearner poet, and I yearn for you. I yearn for you in the way the sea yearns for storms despite knowing how violently the waves will break against themselves afterward. There is something catastrophic about the way I carry your existence inside me, something almost fatal in the way your name settles beneath my ribs like a second heartbeat I cannot remove. People speak of love as though it were gentle, as though it arrived softly with flowers in its hands and mercy in its mouth, but they have never loved the way I do. they have never tasted the kind of longing that turns a body into a graveyard of unfinished prayers. because loving you does not feel holy—it feels like standing barefoot inside a burning cathedral, watching devotion consume itself alive while still calling it beautiful.

Yearning has always been the oldest thing inside me, older than faith. Older than grief. Older even than my own name. it sits beneath my skin like something ancient and starving, something that claws at my ribs every time silence becomes too heavy to survive. i think some people are born untouched by it, allowed to move through the world without carrying this unbearable ache inside their chest, but i was not made that way. I was made to want endlessly. to stand at the edge of absence and still reach my hands toward it as though longing itself could resurrect what has never truly belonged to me.

And perhaps that is the curse of the yearning poet: we do not simply miss people. we become inhabited by them. They linger in us like unfinished scripture, like prayers abandoned halfway through because the mouth could no longer endure the weight of speaking them aloud. Your existence follows me everywhere, not loudly, but persistently, like the sound of distant church bells swallowed by fog. There are moments where I cannot tell whether I am remembering you or worshipping you. Perhaps there is no difference anymore. perhaps yearning itself is a religion built from absence.

I have tried to escape it before. I have tried to tear your name out of my thoughts the same way desperate believers try to purge themselves of sin, but yearning is not something that leaves quietly. It roots itself deeper the more violently you resist it. It spreads through the body like smoke filling the lungs of a burning cathedral, darkening every untouched corner until even breathing begins to feel devoted to it. And still, despite the suffocation, despite the ruin, I continue feeding it. I continue kneeling before it. Because the terrifying thing about yearning is that it hurts, and yet the pain feels sacred enough to keep.

There are nights where I think yearning is holier than fulfillment itself. Because to finally possess something is to watch the hunger end, and I do not know who I am without hunger. The yearner poet survives through the unbearable continuation of wanting. through empty hands that still tremble as though they have touched divinity moments before. Through staring into the darkness and imagining it staring back with your face hidden somewhere inside it. I do not need answers anymore. I do not need certainty. i only need the ache to remain, because without it, the silence inside me would become infinite.

Even heaven and hell begin to lose meaning after enough yearning. Heaven becomes nothing more than a distant promise of closeness, while hell becomes the endless awareness of distance. And I remain suspended somewhere between both, neither forgiven nor condemned, carrying this longing like a wound I have learned to romanticize. I think that is the most blasphemous part of all—not that I yearn, but that I have begun to cherish the suffering of it. I have begun to treat my own devastation like scripture, rereading every ache as though pain itself could bring me nearer to something divine.

Because yearning changes a person. It hollows them out slowly, beautifully, until all that remains is devotion stripped bare of dignity. It turns ordinary things into relics. a voice becomes prophecy. A memory becomes ritual. a name becomes something repeated so often it no longer sounds human, only holy. And I have repeated yours enough times that it now echoes through me like a prayer spoken inside an empty cathedral where no god has answered for centuries.

Still, I remain.

Still, I yearn.

Because I am the yearner poet, and yearning is the only thing that has ever made me feel infinite.


Written in the quiet hours, mostly for me—and now, for you too.
Cathlynn.

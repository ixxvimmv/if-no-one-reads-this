Make A Reading Habit

Not every story is printed on paper.

life

They say, "Make a reading habit.”  

So, I did. 

At first, I thought it meant books. Pages. Chapters. Margins filled with notes and passages underlined in haste because I was afraid I'd forget how they made me feel. But reality has a different curriculum.

When you step outside the comfort of paper and ink, you realize that life requires a different kind of reading. You don’t just read books anymore—you read people. You read the way someone answers a simple question. You read the pauses that stretch a little too long. You read the smiles that don’t quite reach the eyes. You read the things people say and the things they spend a lifetime avoiding.

You learn that every person carries a language of their own.

Some speak through words. Others through actions. And others through silence. Some tell you exactly who they are. Others leave clues scattered everywhere, hoping someone will be patient enough to piece them together.

And so, without noticing, I developed a reading habit.

I began reading with tired eyes and restless hands. I read affection disguised as teasing, grief behind humor, and loneliness buried beneath a crowded room. I read people who wanted to be understood and people who spent years making themselves impossible to understand.

The strange thing is that people are far more complicated than any book I’ve ever held.

A novel eventually reaches its final chapter. Every mystery is solved. Every question is answered. The story ends.

While people don’t.

And that’s why reading people is so difficult.

because we’re all unfinished manuscripts.

drafts upon drafts of who we were, who we are, and who we’re trying to become.

Yet we keep writing.

So when people tell me to make a reading habit, I think I've done exactly that.

I read every day.

Not always from the book, but from the world around me.

from conversations.

from goodbyes.

from fleeting moments.

from the stories people carry within them.

because life, I’ve learned, is an endless library filled with unfinished books. and every person you meet is a story still being written.

including yourself.

Written in the quiet hours, mostly for me—and now, for you too.
Cathlynn.

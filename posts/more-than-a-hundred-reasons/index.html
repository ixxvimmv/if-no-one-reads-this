More Than a Hundred Reasons

I could spend a lifetime counting, and I’d still find another reason to love you.

lettershomelove

There’s this “100 reasons why I love you” trend that has been lurking around my FYP on TikTok lately. I’ve seen people make their own lists, putting into words a hundred little things they adore about someone. Some write about the way that person smiles, some about their little habits, some about the memories they’ve shared, and some about the things that person probably doesn’t even realize they do. I think there’s something beautiful about it—taking all those little pieces of someone and trying to turn them into words.

But if I were to make one for you, I already know I’d have a problem.

I wouldn’t know where to begin, and I definitely wouldn’t know where to end.

Not because I don’t have enough reasons, but because I have too many. A hundred sounds like a lot until I start thinking about you. Then suddenly, it feels like I’m being asked to choose only a handful of stars from an entire sky and pretend that the rest don’t exist.

I could start with the things that are easy to explain. Your smile. Your laugh. Your voice. The way you tell stories. The way you randomly sing in the middle of a conversation. The little expressions you make without realizing it. The way you get excited when you talk about something you love. The way you can turn the most ordinary story into something I actually want to remember.

But even if I wrote all of those down, I would still be nowhere near finished.

Because there are things about you that I love that don’t really feel like reasons.

They’re feelings.

It’s the comfort of seeing your name appear on my phone. It’s knowing that somewhere in the middle of my day, there might be a message from you. It’s the way an ordinary day can suddenly feel lighter because you decided to tell me something random. It’s the strange happiness I get from being included in the smallest parts of your life.

You can tell me about your day, something that happened at school, something that annoyed you, something that made you laugh, something you’re worried about, something you’re studying, or something completely unrelated that suddenly crossed your mind. And somehow, I want to hear all of it.

I never thought I would become someone who could listen to another person talk for hours and genuinely enjoy it. But then there’s you.

You don’t have to make your stories interesting for me to want to listen. You don’t have to have something important to say. You don’t even have to make sense sometimes. You can simply yap about whatever is on your mind, and I’ll still be there, listening.

Maybe that’s what makes being your listener so easy.

I don’t listen because every story has a lesson.

I listen because it’s you telling it.

And I think there’s a difference.

Maybe that’s also why I stopped minding phone calls.

I used to think phone calls were something I could live without. Then you became the exception. Now, I can find myself enjoying the quietest calls—the ones where you’re studying and I’m doing something else, the ones where you’re reviewing for a demo or return demonstration, the ones where you’re doing your makeup, the ones where you’re telling me about your day, and even the ones where neither of us has anything particularly interesting to say.

Sometimes you’re busy doing your own thing while I’m doing mine.

Sometimes the conversation gets quieter.

Sometimes one of us gets sleepy.

And sometimes we don’t even need to fill the silence.

Just knowing you’re there is enough.

I think those are the moments I would struggle to put into a list.

Because how do I number the comfort of your presence?

How do I make a reason out of the feeling of being able to exist beside someone without having to constantly entertain them?

How do I explain the kind of silence that doesn’t feel awkward because it’s shared with the right person?

Those things don’t feel like reasons anymore.

They feel like home.

And that’s when I realize that maybe I was never supposed to count them in the first place.

“A hundred reasons might be enough for a list, but they could never be enough for you. There are still so many things I have yet to discover, so many moments we have yet to share, and so many reasons I haven’t found yet.”

Because there are still parts of you I haven’t discovered.

There are still conversations we haven’t had, places we haven’t gone to, memories we haven’t made, and versions of ourselves we haven’t met yet. There are still countless ordinary days ahead of us that could become meaningful simply because you were there.

And maybe that’s the part I love most about this.

I don’t know every reason yet.

Some of them haven’t happened.

Some of them are still somewhere in the future, waiting for us.

There might be a future version of you who will give me another reason to smile. Another version of you who will surprise me. Another little habit I’ll eventually notice. Another story you’ll tell me. Another moment where I’ll look at you and think, “There you are. Another reason.”

How could I possibly limit something that hasn’t even finished becoming?

Then there’s the way you care for people.

I admire that part of you more than I probably say. You have this instinct to look after others, to notice when someone needs something, to worry about people, and to make sure they’re okay. And while I know you’re becoming a nurse, I don’t think your ability to care started with the dream of becoming one.

I think it was already part of you long before the uniform, the title, the license, or the hospital.

The profession will give that kindness a name, but the heart behind it was already there.

I’ve watched you continue showing up even when you’re tired. I’ve seen how much you give of yourself even when you have your own things to carry. You somehow find room to care about other people while dealing with your own struggles.

And sometimes, I wish you could see yourself the way I see you.

Maybe then you’d understand why I’m so proud of you.

Not simply because you’re becoming a nurse.

Not because one day people will call you “Nurse.”

But because of the person you are becoming along the way.

I think about the little girl you once were—the girl who had dreams before she knew how difficult it would be to reach them. I never got the chance to meet her, but I still feel a strange tenderness toward that version of you.

Because she became you.

Everything she went through, every difficult day, every moment she had to be strong, every time she kept moving even when she was tired, eventually led to the woman I get to know today.

So when I say I’m rooting for the nurse you’re becoming, I don’t only mean the future version of you in scrubs.

I’m rooting for the little girl who dreamed of becoming her, too.

I’m rooting for every version of you that had to keep going.

And when you finally get to where you’ve been trying so hard to go, I hope you remember the person who started the journey long before she knew where it would lead.

I hope you’re proud of her.

Because I am.

Then there are the little things you’ve unintentionally left behind in my life.

You made me notice pink more than I ever used to.

A color became a reminder of you.

Now, whenever I see something pink, there’s this little part of my mind that immediately thinks of you. It could be something completely insignificant—a shirt, a bag, a sign, something on a shelf, something I pass by without a second thought. And suddenly, I think, “That’s her favorite color.”

It’s funny how someone can change the meaning of something so ordinary.

Before you, it was just a color.

Now, somehow, it carries a piece of you.

And it isn’t only pink.

There are songs that sound different because I associate them with you. There are certain words that mean more because I remember you saying them. There are moments that probably would have disappeared from my memory if you weren’t part of them.

That’s what love seems to do.

It leaves traces.

Little pieces of someone begin appearing in places you never expected them to.

And before you know it, someone has found their way into the smallest corners of your everyday life.

You’ve done that to me.

Without trying.

Without asking.

You’ve simply become part of how I see things.

And I think that’s another reason a hundred could never be enough.

Because I’m not only counting the things you do.

I’m also counting the things you’ve changed in me.

You’ve made me softer in places I didn’t realize were hard. You’ve made me more intentional with the way I love. You’ve taught me that affection doesn’t always have to be loud or grand. Sometimes it’s something as simple as asking if someone has eaten. Sometimes it’s remembering a detail they mentioned weeks ago. Sometimes it’s staying on a call a little longer. Sometimes it’s listening when there’s nothing you can do to fix the problem.

Sometimes love is simply saying, “I’m here.”

And I want to learn how to love you in those little ways.

Not only when everything is beautiful.

Especially when it isn’t.

Because I know there will be days when you come home exhausted. Days when school feels overwhelming. Days when your responsibilities pile up. Days when you question yourself. Days when you feel like you’re not doing enough even though you are.

There will be days when you don’t feel like the strongest version of yourself.

And on those days, I don’t want to be another thing you have to carry.

I want to be someone you can put your burdens down with.

Someone you can tell everything to without worrying that you’re being too much.

Someone who can listen without immediately trying to solve everything.

Someone who can sit beside you in the quiet and let you take your time becoming okay again.

Because loving someone isn’t only about loving the version of them that smiles easily.

Sometimes it means staying when the smile disappears.

Sometimes it means learning their silence.

Sometimes it means being patient with the parts of them that are still trying to understand themselves.

And that’s the kind of love I want to give you.

A love that doesn’t require you to always be happy, strong, productive, or certain.

A love that lets you be human.

You’ll make mistakes.

You’ll have bad days.

You’ll get tired.

You’ll become frustrated.

You’ll change your mind.

There will be moments when you don’t recognize yourself, and there will be days when you feel like you’re falling behind.

But I never want those moments to make you question whether you are still worthy of being loved.

You are.

Even there.

Especially there.

Because if I loved you only for the things you did well, then what would happen on the days when you couldn’t?

If I loved you only because you made me happy, what would happen when life became difficult?

If I loved only the version of you I know today, what would happen when tomorrow changes you?

I don’t want my love for you to be a checklist.

I don’t want to love a collection of qualities and call that loving you.

I want to love the person behind all of them.

And people change.

You will change.

I will change.

The things we want, the things we fear, the things we believe, and the way we see the world will continue changing as we grow.

So maybe I don’t want to write down one hundred reasons.

Maybe I want to leave room for the one hundred and first.

And the one hundred and second.

And the five hundredth.

And every reason that doesn’t exist yet because we haven’t lived the moment that will create it.

There are still so many versions of you I haven’t met.

The future you.

The older you.

The you who finally reaches the dreams you’ve been working toward.

The you who becomes more confident.

The you who learns that resting doesn’t mean failing.

The you who looks back at who you are today and realizes just how far you’ve come.

I want to meet all of them.

And I hope that, through every version, I’ll still find something new to love.

Maybe someday, we’ll look back at who we are now and laugh at the things we used to worry about. Maybe we’ll remember these late-night conversations, the random calls, the stories that didn’t really have a point, the little things we thought were ordinary, and realize that those were the moments that quietly became some of our favorites.

Maybe there will be memories we haven’t even imagined yet.

And maybe some of those memories will become reasons I never thought to write down today.

That’s why I could never promise you only a hundred.

I’d rather promise you that I’ll keep noticing.

I’ll keep paying attention to the little things.

I’ll keep learning you.

I’ll keep discovering the parts of you that you haven’t shown me yet.

I’ll keep finding beauty in the ordinary things you do.

And when I think I’ve already found every possible reason, I’ll probably find another one the next day.

I might start a list someday.

I might write the first few reasons.

I might even reach one hundred.

But I know myself well enough to know that when I reach the hundredth one, I’ll probably stare at the page and think, “Wait. I forgot this.”

And then I’ll add another.

Then another.

Then another.

Until the list stops looking like a list and starts looking like a lifetime.

Because maybe that’s what I actually want.

Not a hundred reasons.

A lifetime of reasons.

A lifetime of getting to know you, listening to your stories, laughing at your randomness, supporting your dreams, being there through the difficult days, celebrating the good ones, and meeting every version of you that life allows me to know.

So no, I don’t think I’ll join the “100 reasons why I love you” trend.

One hundred is beautiful.

But one hundred is still a limit.

And I don’t want to put a limit on you.

I don’t want to reach a number and say, “That’s everything.”

Because it isn’t.

There will always be something else.

Another memory.

Another conversation.

Another laugh.

Another ordinary day that somehow becomes special because you were in it.

Another little thing you do without realizing it.

Another reason I didn’t know yesterday.

Another reason I’ll discover tomorrow.

And maybe, after all this time, that will always be my favorite thing about loving you: there will never be an ending to discover.

So if you ever ask me how many reasons I have for loving you, I might never be able to give you an exact number.

I’ll probably just tell you that I stopped counting.

Not because I ran out.

But because there were too many.

Because you became more than a list could hold, more than a number could measure, and more than a hundred reasons could ever explain.

I could spend forever trying to name every reason.

And I think I’d still be writing when forever ends.

Written in the quiet hours, mostly for me—and now, for you too.

Cathlynn.

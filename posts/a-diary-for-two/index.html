A Diary for Two

The pages I never knew would belong to us.

lettershomelove

I didn't know that my website, If No One Reads This, would eventually be filled with letters for you.

It was originally a space entirely for me—my thoughts I couldn't say out loud, feelings and emotions I didn't know what to do with, and a little corner of the internet where I could write simply because I needed to write. It was never meant to be about anyone else. I wasn't thinking about who would read it, who would understand it, or whether anyone would ever find meaning in the words I left there.

Little did I know that you would slowly find your way into those pages.

What started as a website for myself somehow became part of you, too. It became a diary for two, even though I never planned for it to be one. There are letters about how we met, about the night our conversations began, about asking you if you'd let me court you and see where this could go. There are pieces about the person you are becoming, the future version of you I'm curious to meet, and even the little girl you once were—the one I never had the chance to know but somehow still want to understand through the woman you've become.

And somewhere along the way, I realized something about what this website had become:

Maybe I started writing because I needed somewhere to put my thoughts, but somewhere between one page and another, you became part of the reason I kept writing. What was once a place where I only left pieces of myself slowly became a place where I could leave pieces of us, too.

That's the irony of its name, If No One Reads This.

At first, I genuinely didn't care if anyone ever saw these words. I wrote because writing was enough. I didn't need an audience. I didn't need anyone to understand. But then I met someone who made me want these words to reach her.

You.

The website became a timeline of us without me ever meaning for it to. Each new piece became a small marker of where we were, who we were, and what I was feeling at that particular moment. Some entries came from conversations we had. Some came from things you said without realizing how deeply they stayed with me. Some came from moments I wanted to remember because I knew they would eventually become memories.

It's still my website. These are still my thoughts, my words, my little corner of the internet.

But the story behind so many of them belongs to both of us.

And it isn't just the website that somehow became a diary for two. My other accounts did, too.

I never intentionally decided that I wanted my accounts to be filled with you. I didn't sit down one day and think, I'm going to make this account about her. It just happened. A picture here. A video there. A moment I wanted to keep. Something about you that made me want to share it.

And eventually, I realized how much of you had quietly found its way into the spaces I used to keep entirely for myself.

Maybe it's because I want people to see you.

Not just because you're beautiful, but because you're someone I'm proud to have in my life. Someone I look at and think, How lucky am I that I get to know this person?

Sometimes I want other people to see the things I see in you. The way you laugh. The way you care. The little things you probably don't even realize are worth remembering. The person you are when no one is asking you to be anything.

And maybe, in some quiet way, I want people to know how lucky I feel to have you—and how lucky I think we both are to have found each other.

You don't have to appear in every sentence for me to know that you're somewhere in the pages.

Sometimes you're the subject. Sometimes you're the reason a thought exists. Sometimes you're the person I imagine reading it. And sometimes, you're simply part of the moment that made me open my laptop and start writing.

Writing about you doesn't just preserve how I feel about you. It preserves who I was while feeling it.

The version of me who was brave enough to ask if I could court you. The version who was still figuring out what it meant to care about someone this deeply. The version who became curious about your dreams, your past, the person you're becoming, and all the parts of you that existed long before I ever came into your life.

Especially that little girl.

I know I can never meet her exactly as she was. I can't go back to the moments that shaped her or change the things she had to experience. But I can meet her through your stories. Through the memories you share. Through the woman she eventually became.

I want to understand her.

I want to know what she dreamed about, what made her happy, what frightened her, what she thought her future would look like. I want to understand how that little girl grew into the woman sitting on the other side of my conversations now.

Maybe someday, you'll read everything I've written here and see more than just letters.

Maybe you'll see a record of how I slowly came to know you.

What I noticed. What I wondered about. What I hoped for. The things I was too shy to say directly but somehow brave enough to write down.

Because this was never supposed to be a love story I wrote alone.

It started as my website.

My thoughts.

My words.

My little corner of the internet.

But then you happened.

And somehow, you became part of the story that was already being written.

Maybe it is still a place for me. Maybe it always will be.

But somewhere between the pages, between the pictures, between the things I wrote and the things I chose to keep, it became a place for both of us, too.

A diary for two.

Not because we planned it that way.

Not because I ever intended for it to happen.

But because some people enter your life so naturally that one day you look around and realize they've become part of everything.

And maybe that's what you became to me.

Not someone I deliberately wrote into the story.

Just someone who slowly became impossible not to write about.

This website doesn't preserve a promise of how our story will end.

It preserves how it felt while we were still becoming.

While you were becoming you.

While I was becoming me.

And somewhere in between, we were becoming us.

Written in the quiet hours, mostly for me—and now, for you too.
Cathlynn.
